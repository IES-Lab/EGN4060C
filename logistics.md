# Logitstics

The course uses a **flipped-classroom instructional strategy**. Students are expected to review the assigned reading materials before each class so that lectures and discussions can focus on deeper explanations, applications, and problem solving. Course evaluation will be based primarily on quizzes, labs, a team project, and a final exam.

## Reading Assignments

Readings will be assigned for most class sessions. Students are expected to complete the assigned readings before class. Reading assignments will not be graded directly, but completing them is strongly recommended, as lectures, quizzes, discussions, and other course activities will build on the assigned materials.

## Quizzes

At the beginning of selected class sessions, a brief review of the assigned reading may be provided, followed by a quiz covering the reading materials and topics related to the current chapter. Quizzes must be completed by hand and submitted during the designated class period. Late submissions will not be accepted.

## Student Presentations (Optional)

At the end of selected class sessions, students may volunteer to give a short presentation introducing a research paper, recent research result, or emerging technology related to the topics covered in class. The presentation should briefly explain the work and discuss how it connects to the concepts learned in the course.

Participation in these presentations is optional and may earn bonus points toward the final grade, up to 5%.

## Labs

In addition to the lectures, the course includes weekly laboratory sessions. Each lab will typically begin with a short introduction to the concepts, tools, and specific problems addressed in that session. Students will then work on the assigned problems during the scheduled lab period. Lab performance will generally be evaluated based on:

- **Robot demonstration:** Successful demonstration of the required task.
- **Implementation:** Correctness and quality of the technical implementation.
- **Conceptual understanding:** Ability to answer questions about the underlying concepts and techniques covered in the lab.

Students are expected to attend and actively participate in their assigned lab sessions.

## Team Project

Students are required to work in teams of 3–4 students to develop and prototype a creative solution to a robotics problem of their choice. The project should be closely related to the concepts and techniques covered in the course. Students are encouraged to form their teams within the first month of the semester, as project activities will begin toward the end of September.

The team project will include three major milestones:

- **Project Proposal:** Teams will introduce their proposed problem, motivation, and planned approach. This presentation is primarily intended to provide early feedback on the problem formulation and project scope and may not be graded.
- **Progress Review:** Teams will present their technical progress and receive feedback on their approach, technical development, creativity, and relevance to the course.
- **Final Presentation:** Teams will present their completed system and final results. Evaluation will consider the technical quality of the project, demonstrated results, creativity, relevance to the course, and quality of the presentation.

All students are expected to attend the scheduled project presentation sessions, including presentations by other teams.

## Final Exam

A comprehensive final exam will be administered at the end of the semester. The exam may cover material from the entire course, including assigned readings, lectures, quizzes, videos, laboratory concepts, and class discussions.

## Evaluation Rubric

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Quizzes | 30% |
| Labs | 30% |
| Team Project | 30% |
| Final Exam | 10% |
| Optional Student Presentation | Up to 5% bonus |


## Artificial Intelligence (AI) Use Policy

The use of AI tools is prohibited for quizzes and the final exam. For other course activities and assignments, students may use AI tools only through [Microsoft Copilot provided by UCF](https://it.ucf.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2024/02/MS-Copilot-Flyer.pdf).

When AI tools are used, students must clearly disclose how and for what purpose they were used. Students remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, and completeness of all submitted work, regardless of whether AI tools were used.

## Assignment Submission

All assignments, including lab reports and team project materials, must be submitted through Webcourses. Each student may request one extension of up to one week for a lab assignment during the semester for any reason. Except for this one-time extension or an extension explicitly approved by the instructor, late lab assignments will receive a 20% grade reduction per day of lateness. Assignments submitted more than one week after the deadline will receive no credit. Failure to submit any required component of an assignment or team project will result in zero credit for that component.

## Makeup Work Policy

Makeup exams will generally not be provided unless the student submits a request at least 24 hours before the scheduled start of the exam. Makeup exams may be granted for documented circumstances such as:

- University-sponsored activities
- Religious observances
- Legal obligations
- Other circumstances approved by the instructor

Exceptions may be made for unexpected or serious medical emergencies when advance notice is not possible. Medical circumstances must be appropriately documented and should correspond to the date of the scheduled exam.
